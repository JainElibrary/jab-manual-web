## Jain Symbols

Symbols play an important role in religion through their shapes,
meanings, and implications. They are an outward manifestation of deeper
ideology and provide a glimpse into the culture and practice associated
with a religion's basic principles, often offering a simple
understanding of the religious philosophy.

### Jai Jinendra - Greeting

"Jai Jinendra" means, "May the religion established by the Jina prevail
in our hearts." We say this when greeting others because we see an image
of a Jina, destroyer of all inner enemies, in them. Recognizing this, we
bow down with respect to their Soul. Every Soul is capable of becoming a
Jina and overcoming anger, ego, deceit, and greed. A Jina is also known
by the names Arihant and Tirthankar. We consider them as Jain gods.

### Michchhämi Dukkadam - Asking Forgiveness

Michchhämi Dukkadam is a phrase by which one requests forgiveness, It
literally means "Forgive my faults" and is usually spoken after
performing the annual forgiveness and repentance ritual known as
Samvatsari Pratikraman. It is used in day-to-day scenarios for faults
committed knowingly or unknowingly. Ideally, forgiveness should be
requested as soon as one realizes his/her mistake.

### Jinälay - Jain Temple (Deräsar or Mandir)

A Jinälay, Deräsar, or Mandir is a place of worship where one can
experience immense peace and serenity.

The images of Tirthankars, along with the temple's environment, promote
introspection and emphasize the feeling that God resides within one's
own heart. Therefore, each person can be motivated to follow the right
path of purification and free their inner self of anger, ego, deceit,
and greed.

### Om

The symbol common to all religions in India is "Om." In Hindu
philosophy, it consists of three letters, namely A, U, and M. When these
three letters are joined together, the word sounds like Om. It expresses
creative, preservative, and destructive principles. When the letter A is
pronounced, one's breath comes from the throat, which signifies
creation. The letter U keeps one's breath in the mouth for a time and,
therefore, is the symbol of the preservation. Finally, the letter M
stops the breath for a time and lets it out through a different channel,
the nostrils, symbolizing destruction, and regeneration.

Om means completeness. It is a symbolic word meaning infinity,
perfection, and eternity. The very sound is complete, representing the
wholeness of all things.

Upon attaining absolute knowledge or omniscience, the body of the
Arihant emanates Om (called the Divine Sound - Divya Dhvani). It is an
involuntary, spontaneous, and melodious sound that all humans, animals,
and heavenly beings can understand as a sermon in their own languages.
Om sounds like Aum, which is salutation to five supreme beings andis
made up of five sounds: a, a, ä, u, and m:

-   The first letter "a" represents Arihant (a living human being in the
    highest perfected state who has realized the true nature of the soul
    and reality and has conquered passions).

-   The second "a" represents Ashariri (Siddha, a liberated soul who
    does not have a physical body. While the Arihant is living, acting
    as spiritual master, this is a liberated soul after human
    existence.).

-   The third letter "ä" represents Ächärya (an ascetic who heads the
    Jain congregation).

-   The fourth letter "u" represents Upädhyäy (an ascetic teacher).

-   The fifth letter "m" represents Muni (Sädhus or Sädhvis who practice
    Jain principles).

The Om represents a salutation to the five revered personalities in the
Jain religion. Om is also considered a shortened form of the Namokär
Mantra.

### Hrim

The word Hrim is a seed mantra and is also called the Hrimkär mantra. It
is a mystical symbol representing the invisible sound, infinity, and
divine energy of the 24 Tirthankars. While meditating on Hrim, one can
experience the sublimating energy of Tirthankars.

### Arhum

The word Arhum is a mantra representing all vowel and consonant sounds
in the Sanskrit alphabet. The first vowel in the Sanskrit alphabet is
"a" and the last consonant is "h." While meditating on this mantra, one
focuses on the silent sound of the universe.

### Swastik

The Swastik is considered an auspicious and a pious symbol. The arms of
the Swastik represent the four possible states (Gati) of rebirth -
heavenly being, human, hellish being, and animal -- and are represented
clockwise starting from the upper left corner on the Swastik. Our
ultimate aim is liberation from these four states of rebirth. The
Swastik also reminds us that we should become pillars of the four-fold
Jain Sangh. We can achieve this by first striving to be a true Shrävak
or Shrävikä, and after overcoming our social attachments, renounce
worldly life and follow the path of a Sädhu or Sädhvi.

The four arms are also representative of Dän (charity), Sheel (virtue),
Tap (austerities), and Bhäv (noble thoughts). The three dots above the
Swastik represent the three jewels of Jainism -- Samyag Darshan, Samyag
Jnän, and Samyak Chäritra (perfect perception, knowledge, and conduct,
respectively). Together, these represent the Jain path to liberation.

At the top, there is a small crescent symbolizing Siddha-shilä.
Liberated souls reside right above Siddha-shilä, in Siddha-kshetra. The
dot above the crescent represents a Siddha or a liberated soul. In order
to achieve this stage, a soul must shed all its Karma. Every living
being should strive for this state of liberation or Moksh.

### Tilak

People belonging to different religious traditions and beliefs often
make unique marks on their foreheads, called Tilaks. They are typically
made with some kind of fragrant paste. - Jains use sandalwood paste
mixed with saffron. The Tilak is placed between the two eyebrows, either
in a round or almond shape. Physiologically, this is a plexus, or nerve
center, that is also a source of decision-making power. It represents an
inner sight that can see and recognize things that ordinary vision
cannot. This sign indicates that Jains will strive to live their lives
by the tenets of the Tirthankars

### Universal Jain Symbol

This universal Jain symbol is a combination of various symbols, each
with a deeper meaning. It was adopted during the 2500th Nirväna
celebration of Bhagawän Mahävir.

The outline of the symbol is defined as the Universe or Lok. The lower
part of the symbol represents the seven hells (Näraki). The middle part
of the universe contains the Earth and the planets (Manushya-lok). The
upper part contains the heavenly abodes (Devlok) of all the celestial
beings, on top of which is the abode of the Siddhas (Siddha-kshetra).
Jains believe that this universe is eternal -- it was neither created
nor can be destroyed. It is static and will remain at the same location
in the vast empty space of all existence.

The Swastik is explained on the previous page. The word in the center of
the raised hand and wheel is "Ahimsä," or non-violence. This combined
symbol reminds us to stop for a minute and think twice before starting
any activity. This gives us a chance to analyze our thoughts, words, and
deeds to be sure that they will not cause harm.

The wheel in the hand shows that if we are not careful , we will
repeatedly traverse the cycle of birth and death. The text underneath
the symbol, "Parasparopagraho Jivänäm," translates to "Living Beings
(souls) Render Service to One Another."

### Federation of JAINA Logo

The Federation of Jain Associations in North America (JAINA) has adopted
this symbol. The Swastik from the main Jain symbol has been replaced by
the Aum or Om symbol. In the western world, the Swastik is not viewed as
a pious symbol.

### Ärati

The Ärati has 5 lamps. Flames are lit on oil-soaked cotton wicks in each
of the lamps. The Ärati is waved in a circular motion in front of a
Tirthankar's idol at the end of a ritual and at night before the temple
closes. The light symbolizes divinity overcoming negativity, fear, and
ignorance inherent to darkness. By dispelling darkness, the Ärati
signifies the expelling of negativity through virtue, fear through
courage, and ignorance through knowledge.

The 5 lamps symbolize:

-   Panch Parameshthi, or five great personalities (Arihant, Siddha,
    Ächärya, Upädhyäy, Sädhu)

-   Five types of jnän, or knowledge (Mati-jnän, Shrut-jnän,
    Avadhi-jnän, Manah-paryav jnän, Keval-jnän)

-   Panch Mahävrat, or five great vows (Non-Violence, Non-stealing,
    Truthfulness, Celibacy, Non-possession)

### Mangal Deevo

The Mangal Deevo is a lamp with a single oil-soaked wick that is lit and
waved in a circular motion right after Ärati. When the wick is lit, the
face of the Jina is illuminated. In this manner, we hope our hearts are
illuminated with truth and compassion.

The single lamp is also a symbol of Keval-jnän (infinite knowledge) and
of a Siddha (liberated soul).

### Ashta Mangal

A belief in auspicious objects has been a longtime facet of Indian
culture. It is believed that they can bring good luck and happiness to
families and into homes. The Ashta Mangal, or eight auspicious objects,
have been a part of Jain worship from ancient times. These eight unique
objects are typically depicted with rice grains in front of Tirthankar's
idol. Today, you might find them engraved on a wooden or a metal slab in
the temple or hung over the threshold to a home. These Ashta Mangal are
described below.

#### Swastik

The Swastik symbolizes four destinies: a) human beings, b) heavenly
beings, c) hellish beings and d) remainder of living beings (Tiryancha).

Refer to D.1.7 - Swastik. For more information

#### Shrivatsa

Depicted on a Jina's chest, the Shrivatsa is a mark that speaks to the
highest knowledge manifested from the heart of the Jina. It symbolizes
the endless cycle of rebirth.

#### Nandävarta

This is a big Swastik with nine corners. It indicates treasures of nine
kinds, including material, physical, mental, and spiritual wealth.

#### Vardhamänak

Vardhamän means to increase. The Vardhamänak symbolizes increase in
wealth, health, and, most importantly, spiritual progress.

#### Kalash

The Kalash symbolizes all spiritual wealth, as well as a completeness of
knowledge. Its mouth represents eternity, its throat represents losing
old ways, and its base represents adopting new ways.

#### Bhadräsan

Also called a Simhäsan, the Bhadrasan is a throne. It is considered
auspicious since it is sanctified by the feet of theJina.

#### Minyugal

It symbolizes beings rescued from the ocean of misery, which stems from
an earthly existence.

#### Darpan

It symbolizes the true self -- one\'s soul.

### Mäna-sthambha

The Mäna-sthambha is a pillar that can be found in front of Digambar
temples and represents bringing an end to pride. At its apex, it
typically features a four-faced Jina sitting upon a Samavasaran. The
story behind the Mäna-sthambha traces back to when Indrabhuti Gautam,
proud of his knowledge, decided to engage in debate with Bhagawän
Mahävir. At the mere sight of the pillar in front of the Samavasaran,
Gautam Swami's pride and vanity disappeared.
