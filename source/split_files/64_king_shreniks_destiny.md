## King Shrenik's Destiny

Once, in response to King Shrenik's question about his next birth,
Bhagawän Mahävir stated that he will go the hell. On further inquiry
about why, Bhagawän Mahävir pointed out the following incident, due to
which King Shrenik had acquired that type of Äyushya karma.

Once, the king had gone hunting. He spotted a fleeing deer. The king
spurred his horse and pursued it. Ultimately, he located it taking rest
under a tree. He wielded his arrow with all his might. The arrow pierced
the body of the deer, thereby killing the deer, as well as the unborn
child in its body. The arrow emerged out of the animal and then pierced
a tree trunk and came out its other side, too. Instead of regretting
this violence, the king felt proud and elated at his abilities. His
arrogance around this violent deed led to him acquiring the karma of
leading an infernal life.

The king remembered this incident and felt very sorry for what had
happened. Anxious to wipe out that karma, he asked Bhagawän Mahävir what
he could do about the matter. Bhagawän Mahävir pointed out that his
karma was indelible and bound to become operative. Such intense Karma
cannot be erased without bearing the fruits. The law of karma is
supreme, and no one has authority over its operation.

King Shrenik could not reconcile his fate. He repeatedly asked Bhagawän
Mahävir for a way to avert going to hell. Bhagawän Mahävir said that
some methods existed, but that the king would be unable to successfully
execute any of them due to the indelible nature of his karma. Upon the
king's insistence, Bhagawän Mahävir suggested the following four
alternatives:

Four Alternatives

-   There was a highly religious householder, known as Puniä Shrävak, in
    Räjgrihi. He had given up all desires and was leading a spiritual
    life. His livelihood depended on the innocent occupation of making
    cotton slivers. These slivers, known in the Indian language as Puni,
    led to his namesake as Puniä Shrävak. He earned very little but felt
    content with what he gets. He spent most of his time in Sämäyik.
    Bhagawän Mahävir suggested that the king obtain the merit of one
    Sämäyik from Puniä Shrävak. The king thought it was very simple. He
    approached the man and requested him for the merit of only one
    Sämäyik. Puniä said he had no objections, but could not as merits
    and demerits are non-transferable? The king was disappointed.

-   The king had a servant named Kapila. She never gave anyone alms.
    Bhagawän Mahävir suggested that the king make her give alms on at
    least one day. Therefore, the king asked her to do so accordingly.
    She replied saying she would not even hold alms in her hand. The
    king arranged to tie a scoop to her hand and asked her to give alms.
    While doing so, the servant murmured that the king's scoop was
    giving the alms, not her. Thus, this plan also failed.

-   The king had a butcher named Kälsaurik who loved slaughtering
    animals. Bhagawän Mahävir suggested to that the king make sure the
    butcher Kälsaurik did not slaughter on at least one day. This seemed
    very easy to the king. He ordered Kälsaurik to avoid killing the
    next day. The butcher replied that he was so conditioned to kill
    that he could not go one day without killing. The king ordered his
    men to put him in a dry well so he could not commit slaughter.
    However, Kälsaurik could not keep himself from killing, and made
    animals from wet clay in the well and pretended to kill them. Thus,
    the king could not stop that effort and failed.

-   Bhagawän Mahävir then asked the king to observe the restraint of one
    Navakärashi, in which one is not supposed to eat or drink anything
    until 48 minutes after sunrise. The king agreed to do so on the next
    day. In the morning, he went to his favorite garden and saw ripe
    berries. Forgetting the Navakärashi, he thought about eating the
    berries. By merely thinking of eating, he broke the restraint. He
    was unable to complete the last suggestion.

Moral:

This story tells us about how King Shrenik acquired his Äyushya karma,
which was indelible. Once it is acquired, it cannot be changed, no
matter how hard one tries.

Highlights:

-   King Shrenik acquired his indelible Äyushya karma while hunting.

-   When he learned about this, he begged Bhagawän Mahävir to help him
    change his karma.

-   Bhagawän Mahävir knew this was impossible, but indulged King Shrenik
    and suggested 4 alternatives, knowing that King Shrenik would fail.

    -   Buying the merits of one Sämäyik from Puniä Shrävak

    -   Asking his servant Kapila to give alms.

    -   Asking his butcher Kälsaurik to go without slaughtering animals
        for one day.

    -   Observe Navakärashi for a day.
