## Temples of Delwädä

Mount Äbu is a beautiful town on a mountain in the state of Rajasthan.
Within the town, there are two magnificent Jain temples, known as the
Delwädä Temples. The carvings in both of these temples are breathtaking,
and the ones in the marble ceilings are so minute and intricate that it
is difficult to copy the design, even on paper. These temples are
regarded as "poetry in marble."

Vimalshä built the first temple in the 11th century A.D. at a cost of
180 million rupees. The second temple, called Lunaga Vasahi, was built
by two brothers, Vastupäl and Tejpäl, in the memory of their elder
brother. This temple was built in the 13th century A.D. at a cost of 120
million rupees. The stories associated with the architects of these two
temples are presented here.

### Vimalshä

The Solanki dynasty was the golden period for the state of Gujarat, in
which the authority and prosperity of the state reached its peak. The
credit for putting Gujarat in this position mainly goes to the king's
chancellors and commanders, who were at the helm of affairs.
Interestingly, many chancellors and some commanders of that period were
Jains. Vimalshä was one very capable and outstanding commander in
several respects.

King Mulräj, the founder of the Solanki dynasty, had an advisor named
Vir Mahattam. His wife's name was Virmati. They had three sons named
Nedh, Vimal, and Chahil. While they were still very young, their father
renounced worldly life and became a monk. Their mother then went to her
parents' home and raised her sons with love and care. Nedh was very
intelligent and grew up to be a wise, considerate youth. Vimal was bold
and smart. He liked horseback riding and archery. He steadily gained
expertise in those arts, and in due course turned out to be a well-known
equestrian and an accomplished archer.

As her sons grew older, their mother brought them back to the capital
city of Pätan so they could pursue careers of their choices. In line
with their interests, Nedh joined the royal court and Vimal joined the
army. Both quickly rose the ranks and became known for their outstanding
capabilities. Since Vimal was particularly handsome and brave, he
impressed one of Pätan's multimillionaires, who had a beautiful daughter
named Shridevi. He thought Vimal would be a perfect husband for his
daughter. Vimal agreed, and Shridevi and Vimal got married.

Luck continued to favor both the brothers. During the reign of King
Bhimdev, who came to the throne in 1021 A.D., Nedh became chancellor and
Vimal secured the position of commander. Vimal was fortunate to have a
beautiful, loving wife and a high-ranking military position at a
relatively young age. Moreover, he soon won everyone's hearts with his
amicable nature and came to be known as Vimalshä.

Some people at the court could not bear Vimalshä's good fortune. They
were jealous and began to look for his drawbacks. They noticed that
Vimalshä would not bow to anyone, not even the king, before offering
obeisance to the omniscient Jain Tirthankars. Thereupon, they began
telling King Bhimdev that Vimalshä was arrogant due to his position,
even unwilling to bow to the king. They said his ambition knew no bounds
and he might even try to acquire the throne. Unfortunately, Bhimdev
started to believe those courtiers and began to look at Vimalshä with
suspicion.

When Vimalshä learned of the disaffection of King Bhimdev, he decided to
leave Pätan. Accordingly, he went to mount Äbu (which was known as
Chandraväti at that time) with his followers. There, he learned that
Dhandhuk, the chief of Chandraväti, was aspiring to become independent
from the sovereign King Bhimdev. Vimalshä attacked him with the force at
his command. Dhandhuk could not fight him and fled. Vimalshä thus
occupied Chandraväti. He had no ambition to become king and thus took
possession of Chandraväti in the name of King Bhimdev, considering
himself governor.

Vimalshä now happily passed his days at Chandraväti with his wife.
Shridevi was a very affectionate lady and made him happy in every
respect. They had no children. Being religious-minded, however, the
couple considered this to be the consequence of their unwholesome karma.

Once, they happened to meet Dharmaghosh-suri, a well-known Jain Ächärya
of that time. Vimalshä regularly listened to his sermons, which made him
even more religiously oriented. Remembering his involvement in past
wars, he felt very sorry for the violence and sins he had committed and
sincerely repented. Seeing his genuine repentance, the Ächärya asked him
to construct a temple at Chandraväti and make it a center of pilgrimage
to help atone. Vimalshä was pleased to hear this suggestion and decided
to construct a grand temple.

He was also a devotee of Ambikä Devi, the goddess in service of
Tirthankar Neminäth. He decided to invoke her in order to obtain her
blessings. The goddess was pleased with the earnestness of his worship
and asked him what he wanted. He asked both for a son and the ability to
construct a monumental temple at Chandraväti. However, the goddess asked
him to select one of the two. Vimalshä opted for the temple. Goddess
Ambikä granted his wish.

Vimalshä selected a site for the temple on top of a mountain and bought
the land for 45,360,000 gold coins. The foundation stone was laid with
deep faith. However, it was difficult to construct the temple. Artisans
were not locally available, there was no road leading from the foot of
the mountain to the top, and marble had to be brought from a great
distance. Vimalshä was determined to complete the project at any cost.
He made the necessary arrangements to transport materials to the top and
hired the best sculptors in the country to carry out the work.

All possible care was taken to see that the artisans did not face any
difficulties, and the project did not encounter any problems. It took 14
years to finish the project, and the temple was constructed at a cost of
185,300,000 gold coins. The opening ceremony was performed with great
fanfare and enthusiasm under the guidance of Dharmaghosh-suri,
Vardhamän-suri, and other Ächäryas.

It is a spacious marble temple with highly artistic figures in its
domes, arches, and panels. The sculptures are exquisite, with a kind of
accuracy that would be nearly impossible to achieve even with wax. The
fact that the artists did so in marble is marvelous and instantly draws
every visitor's admiration. Such sculptures are not found anywhere else
in the world. It is said that Vimalshä paid the artists in gold. The
artisans collected and brought in the marble dust they had carved for
the day to be weighed. The person in charge would give them gold equal
to the weight of the stone powder!

His generosity and the beauty of the temple have immortalized his name.
It is virtually a wonder of the world. Later, Vimalshä led a Jain Sangh
to the Shatrunjay hill, located in the town of Pälitänä. He constructed
the Vimal-vasahi temple there, which cost 40 million gold coins. It is
located on the way to the main complex on the hill. A small, but equally
exquisite temple, it is popularly known as Bhulbhulämani temple, meaning
"a maze temple." He also constructed the well-known temples of Kumbhäriä
on the Äräur hill by the northern border of Gujarat. Moreover, he is
credited with the construction of a beautiful temple in the capital city
of Pätan.

One successful but highly instructive anecdote is associated with his
later life. It is said that Shridevi had a dream in which she saw a
goddess. The goddess asked her to go to the temple along with her
husband at midnight on a specific date and ask for whatever they wanted.
Both of them were eager to have a son and went to the temple to express
their wish. While they were waiting for midnight to arrive, they felt
thirsty.

Vimalshä therefore went to an adjoining well to fetch water. Inside the
well were steps that went down to water level. Going down the steps to
get the water, someone asked him to pay a toll for taking the water.
Vimalshä was amazed to hear this and asked a toll was required to drink
the water. The person replied that he was a descendent of the person who
built the well. Since he was poor, he was collecting a toll for using
the well.

Vimalshä was taken aback. He asked himself, "What would happen if one
day some of my own descendants tried to collect a toll for the temple
that I have built?" He shuddered at the idea and asked himself again
whether it would be better to remain childless. He shared the incident
with his wife, who concurred with his thinking.

At midnight, when the goddess asked them what they wanted, Shridevi
replied that she no longer wanted a child. Now, the goddess was taken
aback. Vimalshä narrated his experience and confirmed this sentiment.

### Vastupäl and Tejpäl

Two brothers, Vastupäl and Tejpäl, were ministers in the court of King
Vir-Dhaval in Gujarat. Tejpäl was also a very outstanding army
commander. Both brothers came to be known by their valor and
faithfulness. They helped the king conquer enemies and maintain law and
order in the kingdom. Tejpäl's wife, Anupamä-devi, was a wise and smart
woman who always helped her husband with family matters. She was sweet
spoken, and a very religious and compassionate woman.

Tejpäl always respected her opinion. Once, both brothers' families,
along with many others, went on a pilgrimage. They came to a small
village, an area that was not regarded as very safe for pilgrims since
the road was frequented by burglars. Thinking they might meet some
burglars on the way, the brothers decided to bury and hide their wealth
nearby. They started digging and unearthed a big pot of jewels and coins
from the ground, much to their amazement. They simply did not know what
to do with this wealth.

Tejpäl asked Anupamä-devi what to do with this immense wealth.
Anupamä-devi had no hesitation in replying that this wealth had come
from a deep trench, but the right place for it was on a high mountain.
This would spread the glory of Jainism. Thus, they decided to take it to
the top of the mountain and spend it there.

The brothers decided to build a temple on Mt. Äbu, known as the Lunaga
Vasahi Temple. Tirthankar Neminäth Bhagawän's Samavasaran has been
carved in this temple. Tejpäl also dedicated two Gokhla (niches) for two
women, one to commemorate his wife and the other to commemorate his
brother's wife (sister-in-law). These niches are called "Deräni-Jethäni
nä Gokhla" (The wives of two brothers are known as Deräni-Jethäni.
Deräni refers to the younger brother's wife, while Jethäni is the older
brother's wife).

They constructed many more temples, of which only the Delwädä temple and
the Neminäth Temple of Mount Girnär exist today. However, the Lunaga
Vasahi Temple of Mount Äbu is similar to the Vimalshä Temple. Fifty-two
dev kulikäs (subsidiary shrines) have also been constructed on an
elevated platform around the central temple, each with the statue of a
Tirthankar. Elephants were used to carry the marble to Mount Äbu for
temple construction. A place called Hastishälä is also built within the
temple complex to commemorate the contribution of the elephants.

Moral:

The contribution made by Vimalshä, Vastupäl, and Tejpäl to the
architecture of Jain temples is very inspiring. Their dedication to
their religion, their perseverance, and their honesty are praiseworthy.
Their contribution to Jain architecture is part of our great heritage.
