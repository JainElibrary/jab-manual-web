## Abhaykumär and Thief Rohineya

During the time of Bhagawän Mahävir, there was a burglar named Lohkhur.
He lived in a remote cave in the Vaibhärgiri hill near the city of
Räjgrihi. He was a clever robber and never left any traces of his
burglary. He and his wife, Rohini, had a son named Rohineya. As Rohineya
grew up, he learned how to be a burglar, as well, eventually becoming an
expert, even surpassing his father in intelligence.

It was almost impossible to recognize him when he was in disguise, and
if someone pursued him, he could outrun them. He robbed the rich and hid
the treasures in the most unexpected and inaccessible places. He
extended help to the poor from the wealth he accumulated. Many felt
grateful and were pleased with him, and therefore, were not willing to
help government officials track him down.

Lohkhur was now very old and could see that his life was ending. When he
was on his deathbed, he called Rohineya and said that he was very happy
with his expertise in committing burglary. To remain successful, he
advised his son to never listen to the sermons of Bhagawän Mahävir
because his teachings were not advantageous to their profession.
Rohineya promised his father to abide by his advice.

After Lohkhur died, Rohineya increased his burglary, so much so that it
became almost impossible for rich families to ensure the safety of their
property. They were constantly afraid that Rohineya would go to their
homes when they were out and steal their jewelry and other valuables.
Some people asked King Shrenik to take an action and protect them from
Rohineya's burglaries, since police officers had failed to address the
matter. The king therefore asked his most intelligent chief minister,
Abhaykumär, to take charge of arresting Rohineya.

Once, while Rohineya was covertly on his way to Räjgrihi, he had to pass
by Bhagawän Mahävir's assembly hall. He remembered his father's advice
(never listening to Bhagawän Mahävir's sermon) and put his hands over
his ears. Unfortunately, he stepped on a sharp thorn that deeply pierced
his foot. He had to take his hands off his ears to take out the thorn.
During this time, he heard the following words:

-   Human life is the best of all lives. It is possible to attain
    liberation only as a human.

-   Every human being can attain salvation irrespective of caste, creed,
    or color.

-   By virtuous deeds one can gain a life in heaven where all sorts of
    pleasures and happiness exist.

-   When heavenly beings walk their feet do not touch the ground.

-   Their bodies are without shadow, their eyes remain steady, and their
    garlands do not wither.

-   However, the life of a heavenly being does not lead to ultimate
    liberation, which provides eternal bliss and happiness. Therefore,
    heavenly beings crave a human life."

By then, Rohineya had removed the thorn from his foot. He once again
covered his ears with his hands and proceeded towards the city.

In the city, Abhaykumär had secretly posted trained soldiers in disguise
at the gates and at all important locations. He remained watchful, too.
When Rohineya entered the city, a trained soldier recognized him
although he was disguised as a farmer. The soldier sent a message saying
that an unidentified person had entered the city to Abhaykumär, who
became very alert.

As Rohineya passed by, Abhaykumär glanced at him from a secret hideout.
He recognized the burglar in disguise and instructed his men to surround
him. Since Rohineya was very smart, he quickly recognized the danger and
ran towards the city wall. Unfortunately, there were soldiers near the
wall. He was easily apprehended and was put in jail.

The next day, Rohineya was presented in the royal court. As he was in
disguise, it was hard to identify him as the burglar. Abhaykumär felt
sure, but how could the accused be punished without proof of his
identity? When the king asked him about his identity, Rohineya replied
that he was a farmer named Durgachandra from the Shäligräm village. He
had come to Räjgrihi to visit the capital and was returning home when
the security guards apprehended him. Rohineya had planted his assumed
identity with the residents of the village. When inquiries were made in
the village, people confirmed what Rohineya had stated in court.

Abhaykumär had to devise a plan to get Rohineya to confess to the
burglaries. He learned that Rohineya was fond of drinks. He, therefore,
arranged to serve the thief an excessive amount of wine and indulging it
that he got unconscious. While unconscious, Rohineya was cleaned,
dressed in extravagantly perfumed royal garments, and adorned with
valuable jewelry. He was then placed on a luxurious velvet bed of
sandalwood on the top floor of a palatial building.

As Rohineya regained his consciousness, he saw himself in heavenly
surroundings with breathtaking views. The walls, ceiling, and floor were
crystalline. Beautiful maidens were waving scented air with
diamond-studded fans, soft, serene music was heard in the background,
fairy-like dancers swayed with the music, and divine musicians were
preparing for a concert.

Rohineya could not tell where he was. He asked one of the girls where he
was and why they were all lavishly serving him. The girl replied that he
was their new king in heaven. He had attained all the divine comforts,
which now belonged to him. He could live like Indra, the king of heaven,
and enjoy life with heavenly damsels.

"Could this be true for a burglar like me?" he asked himself. However,
he then remembered that he was helpful to the poor and needy, and he was
sure that some god had been just. "Or could this be the plan of
Abhaykumär?" he thought again. It was hard for him to determine the
truth. He decided it was best to wait and see.

After some time, a luxuriously clad person entered with a golden staff
and book in his hands. "Is your new lord awake?" he asked one of the
damsels. The girl replied that their new lord had just awoken and that
they were getting ready to celebrate his arrival in heaven by presenting
the divine concert. "Let me make sure that all preparations pertaining
to his arrival have been completed before you start your concert. Let me
also get some information from him that the heavenly realm needs to
know." As he was saying this, he came to Rohineya. Opening his book, he
asked Rohineya to narrate his deeds from his previous life before he
could enjoy the amenities of heaven.

Meanwhile, Rohineya was looking around. He remembered what he had heard
from Bhagawän Mahävir's sermon about heavenly beings when taking off
hands placed on his ears. He observed the movements of heavenly beings
before him, and noticed that their feet were touching the ground, their
bodies had shadows, and their eyes blinked like human beings. He
immediately figured out this heaven was only an illusion created by
Abhaykumär to gain evidence of his burglaries.

He replied that he gave donations to worthy causes, constructed temples,
went on pilgrimages to holy places, and rendered service to deserving
people in his previous life. The person noted this and asked him to
narrate any wrong deeds he may have indulged in. Rohineya said that he
had scrupulously avoided misdeeds and, therefore, was born in heaven.
Abhaykumär's plan failed and Rohineya was set free as the innocent
farmer he pretended to be.

After being released, Rohineya constantly thought about what had
happened. He realized that what he had accidentally heard from Bhagawän
Mahävir had saved his life. How could his father's advice be right?
Bhagawän Mahävir must be a very great entity. "If those words, which
were accidentally heard, were so helpful, imagine how helpful his
teachings would be?" he asked himself. Had he wasted his years avoiding
the sermons of Mahävir-swämi?

After pondering at length, he decided to go to Bhagawän Mahävir and
serve at his feet. He went to the assembly and humbly requested Bhagawän
Mahävir to accept him as his disciple. He also requested to become a
monk. Mahävir-swämi asked him to disclose his real identity and confess
all his past sins to the king before renouncing his worldly life.

He disclosed his real identity to the king, who was present in the
assembly, and was ready to accept any punishment. He also asked
Abhaykumär to accept all the treasures collected during his burglaries.

Since Rohineya had voluntarily confessed and had willingly returned
everything that he had taken, the king decided to pardon him and
permitted him to become a monk. Rohineya deeply repented for his deeds.
He started observing severe austerities to erase the Karma acquired by
his misdeeds. In his old age, with permission from Bhagawän Mahävir, he
adopted Sanlekhanä (avoiding food and staying in meditation until
death). After his death, he was reborn in heaven.

Moral:

The themes here fare the principles of honesty and non-stealing. One
should not steal from others, even if one intends to donate the stolen
items to the poor. Wrongdoing is still wrongdoing, regardless of how
much good you do in other areas. One must repent in order to destroy the
bad Karma accumulated as a result of wrongdoing.

The second thing we learn from this story is that liberation can only be
attained as a human. Even heavenly beings cannot attain liberation
without being reborn as human beings. So, we should realize how
fortunate we are to have this human birth and make the most of it, while
also washing away as many bad Karma as we can.

Also, note the power of Bhagawän Mahävir's sermon. A few words from his
sermon saved, and ultimately changed, Rohineya's life forever. Imagine
the benefit of listening to all his teachings. Unfortunately, we cannot
listen to his sermons directly, but we have his teachings available to
us through the Ägam. We should study and understand the Ägam to the
greatest extent so our lives can change for the better, just like
Rohineya's.

Highlights:

-   Rohineya was born in a family of thieves and grew up to be the best
    thief.

-   He promised his father to never listen Bhagawän Mahävir but was
    forced to listen due to his fate.

-   Those few words saved his life when Abhaykumär had him apprehended.

-   He realized the value of Bhagawän Mahävir's teachings, took diksha,
    ultimately went to heaven.

-   He also adopted the vow of Sanlekhanä -- avoiding food and staying
    in meditation until death.

-   In Rohineya's case, his päp, or bad Karma were such that they evened
    out with his punya, or good Karma. Hence, he was able to go to
    heaven.
