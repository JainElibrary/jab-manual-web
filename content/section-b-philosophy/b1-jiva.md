# B.1. Jiva (Living Being)

### B.1.1. Introduction
The first and the most important fundamental is Jiva. Jiva means soul or living being. The soul is the only substance that has consciousness. It is the only substance that can think, feel, and experience. It is the only substance that can achieve liberation.

### B.1.2. Characteristics of Jiva
The main characteristic of Jiva is consciousness (Chetanä) or Upayog. Upayog consists of two parts:
1. **Jnän (Knowledge):** The capacity to know.
2. **Darshan (Perception):** The capacity to perceive.

The soul is amurti (formless). It does not have any color, taste, smell, or touch. It is eternal. It was never created and it will never be destroyed. It is the doer of its own karmas and the enjoyer of the fruits of its own karmas.

### B.1.3. Classification of Jiva
Jivas are classified in many ways. The main classification is:
1. **Samsäri (Worldly) Jivas:** These souls are caught in the cycle of birth and death. They are attached to karmas.
2. **Siddha (Liberated) Jivas:** These souls have been liberated from the cycle of birth and death. They are not attached to any karmas. They reside at the top of the universe in a place called Siddha-shilä.

Samsäri Jivas are further classified into two groups:
1. **Sthävar (Immobile) Jivas:** These have only one sense—the sense of touch. They cannot move of their own accord. There are five types:
    * **Prithvikäy (Earth-bodied):** Living beings having earth as their body (e.g., minerals, salt, stones).
    * **Apkäy (Water-bodied):** Living beings having water as their body (e.g., dew, fog, ice, rain).
    * **Tejukäy (Fire-bodied):** Living beings having fire as their body (e.g., flames, lightning, hot ash).
    * **Väyukäy (Air-bodied):** Living beings having air as their body (e.g., wind, cyclones).
    * **Vanaspatikäy (Plant-bodied):** Living beings having plants as their body. These are further divided into Pratyek (one soul in one body) and Sädhäran (many souls in one body, like potatoes or onions).
2. **Trasa (Mobile) Jivas:** These have two to five senses. They can move away from danger.
    * **Beindriya (Two-sensed):** Touch and Taste (e.g., shells, worms).
    * **Treindriya (Three-sensed):** Touch, Taste, and Smell (e.g., ants, lice, bedbugs).
    * **Churendriya (Four-sensed):** Touch, Taste, Smell, and Sight (e.g., flies, bees, spiders).
    * **Panchendriya (Five-sensed):** Touch, Taste, Smell, Sight, and Hearing (e.g., humans, animals, heavenly beings, hellish beings).
