# B.6. Nav Tattva Part I: Introduction, Äsrav, Bandh

### B.6.1. Introduction
Nav-tattva or nine fundamentals are the essence of Jain philosophy. They deal with the theory of karma, which provides the basis for the path of liberation. Without proper knowledge of these Tattvas, a person cannot progress spiritually.

| # | Tattva | Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Jiva | Soul or living being (Consciousness) |
| 2 | Ajiva | Non-living substances |
| 3 | Äsrav | Influx of karma |
| 4 | Bandh | Bondage of karma |
| 5 | Punya | Virtue |
| 6 | Päp | Sin |
| 7 | Samvar | Stoppage of the influx of karma |
| 8 | Nirjarä | Partial eradication of the accumulated karma |
| 9 | Moksh | Total liberation from karma |

### B.6.6. Äsrav (Inflow of Karma)
Äsrav is the door through which karma enters the soul. It is caused by the activities of mind, speech, and body. When these activities are accompanied by passions (Kashäy), they lead to the bondage of karma.

### B.6.7. Bandh (Bondage of Karma)
Bandh is the process where karma particles become attached to the soul. 
**Four Characteristics of Bondage:**
1. **Prakriti (Nature):** The type of karma.
2. **Sthiti (Duration):** How long the karma will stay with the soul.
3. **Anubhäg (Intensity):** The strength of the fruit of the karma.
4. **Pradesh (Quantity):** The amount of karma particles attached.
