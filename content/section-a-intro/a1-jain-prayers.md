# A.1. Jain Prayers

### A.1.1. Namaskär Mahämangal Sutra

| Sanskrit/Prakrit | Transliteration |
| :--- | :--- |
| नमो अरिहंताणं। | Namo Arihantänam |
| नमो सिद्धाणं। | Namo Siddhänam |
| नमो आयरियाणं। | Namo Äyariyänam |
| नमो उवज्झायाणं। | Namo Uvajjhäyänam |
| नमो लोए सव्वसाहूणं। | Namo Loe Savva Sähunam |
| एसो पंच नमुक्कारो। | Eso Pancha Namukkäro |
| सव्वपावप्पणासणो। | Savva Pävappanäsano |
| मंगलाणं च सव्वेसिं | Mangalänam cha Savvesim |
| पढमं हवइ मंगलं।। | Padhamam Havai Mangalam |

**Meaning:**
[cite_start] I bow to all Arihant, I bow to all Siddha, I bow to all the Ächärya, I bow to the Upädhyäy, I bow to all the Sädhu and Sädhvi [cite: 1, 3]. 

To these five types of great souls, I offer my prayers. [cite_start] May such prayer help diminish all my negative vibrations and sins [cite: 1, 3]. [cite_start] Amongst all of the auspicious benedictions, offering this prayer is the foremost [cite: 1, 3].

### A.1.2. Mängalik - Chattäri Mangalam : (Divine Refuge Prayer)

चत्तारि मंगलं, अरिहंता मंगलं, सिद्धा मंगलं, साहू मंगलं, केवलिपण्णत्तो धम्मो मंगलं .1.
[cite_start]Chattäri mangalam, Arihantä mangalam, Siddhä mangalam, Sähu mangalam, Kevali pannatto dhammo mangalam .1. [cite: 1, 3]

There are four auspicious entities in the universe. Arihants are auspicious. Siddhas are auspicious. Sädhus and Sädhvis are auspicious. [cite_start]The religion explained by the omniscient is auspicious[cite: 1, 3].

चत्तारि लोगुत्तमा, अरिहंता लोगुत्तमा, सिद्धा लोगुत्तमा, साहू लोगुत्तमा, केवलिपण्णत्तो धम्मो लोगुत्त्मो .2.
[cite_start]Chattäri loguttamä, Arihantä loguttamä, Siddhä loguttamä, Sähu loguttamä, Kevali pannatto dhammo loguttamo .2. [cite: 1, 3]

There are four supreme entities in the universe. Arihants are supreme. Siddhas are supreme. Sädhus and Sädhvis are supreme. [cite_start]The religion explained by the omniscient is supreme[cite: 1, 3].

चत्तारि सरणं पवज्जामि, अरिहंते सरणं पवज्जामि, सिद्धे सरणं पवज्जामि, साहू सरणं पवज्जामि, केवलि पण्णत्तं धम्मं सरणं पवज्जामि।।
[cite_start]Chattäri saranam pavajjämi, Arihante saranam pavajjämi, Siddhe saranam pavajjämi, Sähu saranam pavajjämi, Kevali pannattam dhammam saranam pavajjämi || [cite: 1, 3]

I take refuge in the four auspicious and supreme entities of perfected souls, liberated souls, ascetics, and the religion expounded by Tirthankar Kevalis. [cite_start]These are expounded by self-control, non-violence, and compassion[cite: 1, 3].

### A.1.3. Khämemi Savva Jive: (Universal Forgiveness Prayer)

[cite_start]By means of this sutra, we ask for forgiveness from all living beings of the universe, and we also grant forgiveness to all living beings of the universe[cite: 1, 3]. In this way a relationship of mutual forgiveness and friendship is developed among all living beings. [cite_start]This is the true essence of the Jain religion[cite: 1, 3].

खामेमि सव्व जीवे, सव्वेजीवा खमंतुमे। मित्ती मे सव्व भूएसु, वेरम् मज्झ न केणइ।।.1.
[cite_start]Khämemi savva jive, savve jivä khamantu me, Mitti me savva bhuyesu, veram majjha na kenai .1. [cite: 1, 3]

I forgive all living beings, may all living beings forgive me. [cite_start]My friendship is with all living beings, my enmity is nonexistent[cite: 1, 3].

### A.1.4. Upsargäh Kshayam Yänti: (Reflection on Universal Peace)

उपसर्गाः क्षयं यान्ति, छिद्यन्ते विघ्नवल्लयः। मनः प्रसन्न तामेति, पूज्यमाने जिनेश्वरे।। .1.
Upasargäh kshayam yänti, chidyante vighna-vallayah. [cite_start]Manah prasannatämeti, pujyamäne jineshvare .1. [cite: 1, 3]

All problems get resolved; All obstacles get removed. [cite_start]The heart becomes full of joy, for those who get in touch with the inner higher self[cite: 1, 3].

### A.1.5. Shivmastu Sarva Jagatah: (Reflection on Universal Friendship)

शिव मस्तु सर्वजगतः, परहितनिरता भवन्तु भूतगणाः। दोषाः प्रयांतु नाशं, सर्वत्र सुखी भवतु लोकः।। .1.
[cite_start]Shivmastu sarva jagatah, Parhit niratä bhavantu bhutaganäh, Doshäh prayäntu näsham, Sarvatra sukhi bhavatu lokäh .1. [cite: 1, 3]

[cite_start]May the whole universe be blessed, May all beings engage in each other's well-being, May all weakness, sickness and faults diminish and vanish, May everyone be healthy, prosperous, blissful, and peaceful[cite: 1, 3].

### A.1.6. Religious Prayers

**A.1.6.1. Mangalam Bhagawän Viro**
मगल भगवान वीिो, मगल गौतम प्रभ। मगल स्थसलभद्राद्या, जन धमोस्त मगल।।
Mangalam Bhagawän Viro, mangalam Gautam prabhu | [cite_start]Mangalam Sthulibhadrädyä, Jain dharmostu mangalam || [cite: 3]
*Shvetämbar version* - Bhagawän Mahävir is auspicious, Ganadhar Gautam Swämi is auspicious; Ächärya Sthulibhadra is auspicious; [cite_start]Jain religion is auspicious[cite: 3].

मगल भगवान वीिो, मगल गौतमो गसण। मगल कन्दकन्दायो, जन धमोस्त मगल।।
Mangalam Bhagawän Viro, mangalam Gautamo gani | [cite_start]Mangalam Kundakundäryo, Jain dharmostu mangalam || [cite: 3]
*Digambar version* - Bhagawän Mahävir is auspicious, Ganadhar Gautam Swämi is auspicious; Ächärya Kundkund is auspicious; [cite_start]Jain religion is auspicious[cite: 3].

**A.1.6.2. Arhanto Bhagavanta Indramahitäh**
अहिो भगवत इन्द्र मसहता , सिद्धाश्च सिन्तद्ध न्तस्थता। आचाया सजनिािनोन्नसतकिा , पज्या उपाध्यायका । श्री सिद्धाििपाठका मसनविा, ित्नत्रयािाधका । पच त पिमसिन प्रसतसदनम, कवत वो मगलम।।
Arhanto bhagavanta indramahitäh, Siddhäshcha siddhisthitä | Ächäryä jinashäsanonnatikaräh, püjyä Upädhyäyakäh | Shri siddhäntasupäthakä Munivarä, ratnatrayärädhakäh | [cite_start]Panchai te Parameshthinah pratidinam, kurvantu vo mangalam || [cite: 3]

Tirthankar Bhagawän, who is worshipped by heavenly gods; Siddha Bhagawän, who permanently reside above Siddha-shilä; [cite_start]Ächärya Mahäräj, who propagate the Jain religion; revered Upädhyäy Mahäräj; and Sädhus and Sädhvis who are well versed in the scriptures and followers of three jewels of Jainism; may these five supreme beings bestow bliss every day[cite: 3].

**A.1.6.3. Ädimam Pruthivinatha-Mädimam**
आसदम पसिवीनाि-मासदम सनष्परिग्रहम। आसदम तीिनािच ऋषभस्वासमन स्तम ।।
Ädimam pruthivinatha-mädimam nishparigraham | [cite_start]Ädimam tirthanätham cha, Rushabhasväminam stumah || [cite: 3]
[cite_start]We pray to Bhagawän Rishabhadev who was the first king, who was the first one to renounce all his possessions and who is the first Tirthankar[cite: 3, 4].
